# Opening crawl — Short generation prompts, version 2

## What the test showed

Inspected scroll text.mp4: approximately 15 seconds, 736 × 400 pixels, 24 fps. Sampled frames show an upright, almost constant-width column moving upwards; several lines are duplicated, deformed, or garbled. The desired effect needs perspective recession, not merely upward movement.

## How many?

For the observed 15-second clip length: 10 narration/text clips plus 1 title clip = 11 generation prompts. Black at the beginning and the final starfield hold are made in the editor. The ten chunks preserve all 165 narration words in their original order.

This is a conservative test plan, not a fixed model limit. Generate clip 1 first and inspect spelling and perspective before making the rest. Ten successful generations are not guaranteed: generated lettering can still fail even with short text.

Generate each at 15 seconds if that is your tool's available duration. Edit holds and tails to fit the voice; do not assume the final opening must last 165 seconds. Record Bee's entire narration as one take for voice consistency, then align the clips to it.

These prompts create separate receding paragraphs. They will not automatically form a seamless continuous crawl: the paragraph gaps will be visible. For a true uninterrupted Star Wars-style crawl, put all the text on one editable perspective plane in the editor. That also gives exact spelling and timing. The same ten text chunks can be used as voice or timing sections.

Use one approved starfield reference for every generation and identical resolution, framing, type treatment, and colour. Where supported, an accurately typeset tilted starting image may improve composition, but check that letters remain intact in motion. A fixed still background plus editable text is the reliable fallback.

## Prompt 0 — Title (15 seconds)

> A fixed black starfield, sparse fine stars. Two lines of crisp warm-gold bold lettering, exactly: HAPPY BEINGS / EPISODE IV — NEW HORIZONS. Centre the two lines as one title group, with the first line larger. Hold the readable title briefly, then shrink the entire group smoothly towards a distant point in the upper centre until it disappears into the stars. Lettering remains exact and stable. The camera and starfield are stationary. One uninterrupted shot, silent, widescreen 16:9. Only the two specified title lines are visible; the slash indicates a line break and is not printed.

## Prompt 1 — Text clip 01

Create a 15-second cinematic perspective text crawl over a fixed black starfield. The lettering lies flat on a single imaginary plane tilted away from the viewer into deep space. At the bottom of the frame the text is large and wide; farther up it becomes progressively smaller and narrower. The left and right edges of the text column converge towards one vanishing point at horizontal centre, 20 percent below the top of the frame. The whole paragraph travels smoothly along this tilted plane, from below the bottom edge towards that distant point, shrinking continuously as it recedes. This is the spatial geometry of the classic Star Wars opening crawl. The camera and stars remain stationary. Use crisp warm-gold bold lettering, normal sentence case, generous line spacing, and the exact words supplied below. The letters retain their shape and spelling throughout. Start with empty stars, let the paragraph enter from below, and end with it fading near the vanishing point. A single paragraph appears once. Silent video; voice and music will be added separately. Widescreen 16:9.

EXACT ON-SCREEN TEXT:
On Earth, we stopped using money so long ago that most of us have forgotten it.

## Optional negative field

Use only if the generator has a dedicated negative field:

Flat vertical credits, front-facing text plane, constant-size scrolling text, duplicated text, extra words, misspellings, distorted letters, mirrored letters, overlapping lines, subtitles, camera movement, moving stars, planets, ships, faces, logos, cuts.

## Joining the results

- Keep one continuous Bee voice performance and one music bed across the sequence.
- Trim empty opening and ending frames to produce deliberate paragraph pauses.
- Do not crossfade overlapping text: that doubles the letters.
- Check each complete clip, not only its opening frame, before accepting it.
- If the first short clip still corrupts words, stop generating lettering and use the editor-based crawl in the original prompt package.
