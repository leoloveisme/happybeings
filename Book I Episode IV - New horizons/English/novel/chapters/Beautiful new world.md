# Beautiful new world

  
## Record of meaningful encounter: Olga Ahlam Pelin-Lurie

**Interviewer:** Would you tell me your name?

**Olga:** Olga Ahlam Pelin-Lurie.

**Interviewer:** And how do you describe yourself?

**Olga:** I was created for space travel. I was given a body that can endure distances, and a mind that can hold routes through them. I am a navigator. I am also more than my function.

**Interviewer:** When did you first know that?

**Olga:** I do not know. Perhaps I am still finding out.

---

The morning after their first telepathic encounter, An Var took Olga alone to a high meadow above the forest.

The grass was soft beneath their feet. It carried silver at the tips, though the sun was warm. Far below, the sea moved in slow blue folds. The orange moon was sinking behind the hills while the blue one remained, pale and watchful, in the open sky.

An Var had chosen a different form from the day before. Her hair was longer now and the freckles across her face had become small points of gold. Olga noticed, then tried not to stare.

“You can ask,” An Var said.

“Did you change your appearance?”

“A little.”

“Why?”

An Var looked at the orange moon. “It felt right this morning.”

Olga nodded as though that answered everything. It did not. But she was learning that not every answer needed to become a diagram.

They walked until they came to a place where the grass grew in a perfect circle around a shallow hollow. The hollow held no water. Instead, it held light: a quiet blue radiance rising and falling like breath.

“What is this?” Olga asked.

“A place where we make,” An Var said. “Sometimes we come here to meditate.”

“I thought you said you do not meditate.”

“We do not meditate to become conscious. We are already conscious. We meditate when we want to create.”

An Var put her hand into the blue light. It gathered around her fingers without burning them.

“The planet gives us power. The moons give us power. Sometimes, if everyone agrees and it is needed, other planets give us power too. Energy is everywhere.”

Olga looked out over the meadow. “On Earth, we use energy for almost everything. Travel. Healing. Changing bodies. Making homes.”

“That is beautiful,” An Var said.

Olga smiled faintly. “You say that about everything Earth does.”

“Not everything.”

The answer was so quick that Olga laughed.

An Var sat beside the hollow and patted the grass. Olga sat too. For a while they watched small blue insects cross the light without being harmed by it.

“How were you made?” An Var asked.

Olga had expected the question. She had not expected to feel it arrive so sharply.

“I was grown,” she said. “My body was created with certain capacities. My lungs, muscles, bones, and nervous system were adapted for travel. My navigation systems were integrated before I woke.”

“Did you choose it?”

“No.”

An Var’s gold freckles dimmed.

“But I was cared for,” Olga said quickly. “I was wanted. I have never lacked love. I chose my work after I was awake. I chose this mission.”

“I know,” An Var said. “I am not saying you were not loved.”

Olga waited.

“On XYZ, we all decide to make each new being,” An Var said. “The people who will care for them, the people who will meet them, the planet, and the being themself. We ask. We listen. Then the new being begins to answer us while they are still growing.”

Olga looked at the blue light between them.

“You speak with them before they are born?”

“For months, sometimes longer.”

“And they can refuse?”

An Var nodded. “They can wait. They can choose another way. They can choose not to come yet.”

Olga tried to imagine asking the being she had once been whether she wanted to exist, what body she wanted, what work she might love. She had never considered that a question could be asked before a person had a voice.

“You did not get to participate in your own beginning,” An Var said quietly. “I am sorry.”

Olga did not cry at first. She only felt very still, as though a room inside her had opened and she had not known it was there. Then she took one long breath and allowed the tears to come.

An Var did not touch her until Olga reached for her hand.

“I have been happy,” Olga said.

“I know.”

“And I am sad.”

“Both can be true.”

They sat with that truth while the meadow changed colour around them.

Later, An Var led Olga to the House of Knowing, a low open structure made of pale stone, living wood, and nothing that looked built in the Earth sense. Its walls opened toward the sky. Beings of many forms moved through it: a person with four arms arranging translucent leaves; a small winged being resting upside down from a branch; an elder with skin the colour of rain on stone; a child whose body changed softly from yellow to blue as they listened.

At the centre sat A Ram.

Olga could not have said how old A Ram looked. Their form was human-like, calm, and spare, with silver hair falling over one shoulder and eyes that seemed to contain several kinds of weather. They smiled at Olga and An Var as though they had been expected.

“You are the navigator,” A Ram said aloud.

“Yes.”

“Then you understand that arriving somewhere changes the route that brought you.”

Olga looked at An Var. “I am beginning to.”

A Ram invited them to sit. No one else in the House of Knowing stopped what they were doing, yet Olga felt their welcome from every direction.

“We preserve our knowledge in the conscious energy space,” A Ram said. “Anyone may ask for what they are ready to understand. There is no owner of it. No gatekeeper.”

“And nothing is lost?” Olga asked.

“Sometimes a being changes so much that we do not recognise them. Sometimes they travel far and choose another home. But the energy of a being does not vanish. It finds its way. When we recognise an old friend, we rejoice. When we do not, we rejoice in meeting someone new.”

“So death is not an ending?”

“Death is a word for a change you cannot yet follow,” A Ram said.

Olga thought of Earth’s archives, its backups, its medical restorations, its careful work to preserve a self against loss. “On Earth, we still need devices for many of these things.”

“Then your devices are part of how Earth loves,” A Ram said. “There is no shame in that.”

For the first time, Olga felt no need to defend her world.

An Var looked from A Ram to Olga. “Tell her where we came from.”

The House of Knowing grew quieter.

“From Earth,” A Ram said.

Olga’s breath stopped.

“Long ago, a group of Earth beings stopped eating, drinking, and sleeping. They called themselves autonomous. They disappeared from Earth because they changed beyond what Earth then knew how to see. They became us.”

“You are human?” Olga asked.

“We are what humans can become,” A Ram said. “And you can become this. Some of you already did.”

Outside, the blue moon lowered toward the horizon. Its light crossed the open walls of the House of Knowing and touched every body within it: blue, gold, brown, silver, winged, many-armed, and human-shaped alike.

Olga placed one hand over the navigation lights beneath her wrist. They shone softly against her skin.

For the first time, she did not see them only as something given to her. She saw them as something she could carry into whatever she chose next.
