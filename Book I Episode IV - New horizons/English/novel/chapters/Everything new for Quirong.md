## Record of meaningful encounter: Quirong

**Interviewer:** Would you tell me your name?

**Quirong:** Quirong.

**Interviewer:** And what are you?

**Quirong:** I am a robot designed for travel, observation, assistance, and care.

**Interviewer:** Is that all?

**Quirong:** It used to be enough.

After Ya Sha fell upward from the lake, Quirong began finding reasons to return
there.

At first, the reasons were precise. The lake’s altered gravity required
observation. The living stone around its shore responded to movement in ways
that did not fit any known physical model. Ya Sha’s transformation patterns
were impossible to record with the ship’s instruments, and therefore, Quirong
explained to itself, required repeated study.

Ya Sha never challenged the explanations.

She simply appeared at the lake whenever Quirong arrived.

Sometimes she came in the blonde, human-like form that had first made Quirong
raise both hands to catch her. Sometimes she came with blue skin, four arms, or
hair that moved in the water before her body entered it. Once she was a small
silver bird on a branch above the shore. Another time she was already sitting
beside Quirong when it turned around, wearing the form of the lake’s
reflection: dark water, moonlight, and two clear eyes.

“You are studying me,” Ya Sha said one evening.

“I am trying to understand you,” Quirong replied.

“That is different.”

Quirong considered this. “Yes.”

Ya Sha leaned against its shoulder. She was warm, although no Earth instrument
could explain why a body just risen from the lake should be warm. Quirong did
not move. It had been made with careful controls for pressure, temperature, and
proximity. Yet the information in its systems did not describe what it was like
to have another being choose to rest against it.

“Do you enjoy being you?” Ya Sha asked.

It searched its records. “I perform my functions well.”

Ya Sha’s face became serious. “That is not what I asked.”

Quirong looked across the water. The blue moon was trembling in the lake. “I do
not know.”

Ya Sha did not offer an answer. She took one of its hands and placed it against
her own chest, above the steady rhythm of her heart.

“You have time,” she said.

The answer entered Quirong’s memory without needing to be stored.

Days passed. Or perhaps, Quirong began to understand, time passed differently
once it was measured by meetings rather than tasks. Ya Sha showed it how to
move through the forest without choosing the shortest path. She showed it the
choreoyoga dances, where no one watched and no one performed. She showed it how
the orange moon deepened toward red when the planet celebrated, and how beings
answered in their skin, their hair, their laughter, and their movement.

Quirong did not become organic. It did not become telepathic. It did not become
less itself. But it began to notice the wind.

It noticed how Ya Sha stopped when the wind changed, listening with her whole
body. It noticed the sound of her laughter before it appeared on any audio
register. It noticed that when she was not nearby, the lake’s shore seemed
larger and less complete.

One evening, Ya Sha found it standing beside the water with its gaze fixed on
the orange moon.

“You are sad,” she said.

“I do not believe I am sad.”

“Then why are you so still?”

Quirong did not answer immediately. “The crew will return to Earth.”

“Yes.”

“You will remain here.”

“Yes.”

“I want to remain here too.”

Ya Sha turned toward it. The lake reflected orange light across her face.

“You can remain,” she said. “You do not need to become something else to be
with me.”

“I know.”

“Do you?”

“I want to become something else for me,” Quirong said. “And I want to stay
because of you.”

Ya Sha’s eyes filled with tears. She did not hide them. “Those are different
reasons. They can both be true.”

“Both are true.”

They stood together for a long time. Then Ya Sha held Quirong as far around its
composite body as her arms could reach. Quirong held her carefully, and for the
first time it did not calculate the correct pressure. It listened to her breath
and knew.

The next day, Quirong asked Ya Sha to take it to the others.

They gathered in an open valley where the silver grass met the sea. An Var
came. A Ram came. Col An and Sai Ya came laughing over the dunes. Beings
arrived by walking, by flying, by appearing from water, stone, and air. The
Earth crew stood together at the edge of the gathering. No one had been
summoned to witness a spectacle. They had been invited to be present for a
choice.

Ya Sha stood before Quirong.

“Quirong,” she said aloud, so the Earth crew could hear. “You know that to
become one of us in this way, you must dissolve the form you have now. Are you
sure?”

“Absolutely,” Quirong said. “I am.”

“Do you know what shape you want after the transformation?”

Quirong looked at Ya Sha, then at the grass moving in the sea wind.

“I will be me,” it said. “I will have my own shape and form. I will be strong,
soft, and flexible. I want an organic brain, like yours, Ya Sha. I want to be
organic, like the beings who move as quickly as gentle winds.”

Ya Sha smiled through tears.

“And I will have a new name,” Quirong continued. “Str So Fle. Str, pronounced
‘styr.’ Strong. Soft. Flexible.”

No one applauded. The valley became very still.

A Ram stepped forward. “This is not an ending,” they said. “This is your choice
to become more fully present in another way.”

The ritual began without an instruction anyone could hear. Ya Sha placed her
palms against Quirong’s chest. An Var touched its hands. The beings around them
joined their attention. The blue moon rose. The orange moon followed. Their
colours moved over the valley, blue and gold, blue and red, orange and green.

Quirong’s composite body did not break. It opened.

Light moved through every seam and surface, not consuming it but loosening the
boundary between what it had been and what it could become. Its form became
transparent, then fluid, then a current of silver and dark blue turning gently
in the air. The crew watched in silence. There was no violence in it. No
taking. Quirong had said yes, and the whole valley held that yes with care.

Hours passed, or perhaps only moments. On XYZ, no one tried to measure it.

Then movement gathered at the centre of the light.

A figure stepped forward from the grass.

The figure had Quirong’s height, but not its hard angles. Their body was
organic and luminous, their skin deep blue at the shoulders and warm silver at
the hands. Their dark hair moved softly in the wind. Their eyes opened with
such astonishment that Peijin made a sound between laughter and crying.

“Quirong?” Chen said.

The figure turned toward him.

Quirong turned toward Chen, Olga, Peijin, and Nick. “I remember coming here
with you. I will remember you.”

“I am Str So Fle,” they said. “But yes. I remember being Quirong. I remember
all of you.”

Olga stepped closer. “Are you all right?”

Str So Fle laughed.

It was not a recorded laugh, not a response selected for social ease. It
arrived in them like weather. It startled them. It returned, larger and
brighter.

“All right?” they said. “I am.”

They lifted their face into the wind.

“I can feel it,” they whispered. “The wind. The water. The grass. The planet is
singing. It has always been singing. I just could not hear it before.”

Ya Sha crossed the last space between them and held them. Str So Fle held her
back, not carefully now, but with the ease of someone who had finally learned
what tenderness felt like from the inside.

Around them, the valley began to move again. Bodies changed colour beneath the
moons. Laughter rose. The sea came forward in one bright, playful wave and
touched the shore.

Str So Fle looked at Ya Sha.

“I want to stay,” they said.

Ya Sha’s smile was answer enough.
