# Back to Earth / Back on Earth

Earth appeared first as a blue brightness in the dark.

Then it became an ocean, a sweep of white cloud, the curve of a home so
familiar that every person on *Second Horizon* fell silent.

Chen stood at the viewing glass with An Var beside him. She had watched Earth
grow larger for hours. She did not ask many questions. Her amber eyes followed
the movement of weather across the planet, the slow shadows of night, the thin
thread of sunlight reaching the floating cities.

“It is beautiful,” she said at last.

Chen smiled. “It is.”

An Var placed her palm against the glass. “I can feel how hard you are all
trying.”

The words were gentle. That made them harder to receive.

Below them, the harbour city opened its landing field. It was one of Earth’s
round cities, built in widening circles over the sea: gardens, workshops,
homes, Houses of meaningful encounters, and quiet transit lines joining
district to district. From above, it looked almost like a flower floating on
water.

Olga guided the ship down with the steady attention that had brought them
safely across the distance twice. Nick stood beside her at the controls, though
the ship no longer needed his help. Peijin held An Var’s hand. Chen watched the
city and tried to see it as An Var did: not as the place he knew, but as a
living experiment in becoming.

The landing was soft.

When the ramp opened, warm Earth air came inside. It smelled of rain, stone,
seaweed, and the green roofs of the city. A crowd waited beyond the harbour
line, not held back by barriers but gathered in a wide, respectful circle.
People had come to welcome the missing crew, the returning expedition, and the
travellers who had gone out searching.

Chen saw his nephew before anyone else.

The child stood at the front with both hands clasped beneath their chin, trying
very hard not to run forward too soon. When Chen appeared at the top of the
ramp, the child failed completely and came racing across the landing field.

Chen knelt and caught them.

“You came back,” his nephew said into his shoulder.

“I did.”

The child pulled away and looked at An Var. “Is this someone from the other
planet?”

An Var studied the child with the same open attention she gave the ocean and
the stars.

“Yes,” she said. “I am An Var.”

“I am Chen’s nephew.” // he needs a name

“I know,” An Var said. “You gave him a pebble.”

The child’s eyes grew wide. “Did he keep it?”

Chen reached into his pocket. The pebble was there, worn smooth by the journey.
An Var placed it in the child’s hand.

“He kept it,” she said. “And he brought it home.”

The crowd did not cheer immediately. They watched An Var, curious and
uncertain, trying to understand what they were welcoming. She was naked,
blue-and-gold in the changing afternoon light, small by Earth measure and two
hundred years old by her own. She had no luggage. She needed neither food nor
water. She looked at the city as though every bridge, every garden, and every
face was a new kind of star.

Then one of the returned androids from *First Horizon* stepped down the ramp.
The circle opened for them. Someone began to cry. Someone else began to laugh
in relief. The welcome became movement: embraces, names being called, hands
held, faces touched, space made for those who needed it.

An Var watched everything.

“Your people are very good at coming back together,” she said to Peijin.

“We have had a lot of practice,” Peijin replied.

“That can be sad.”

“Sometimes.”

“And beautiful?”

Peijin looked across the harbour at the reunions. “Also beautiful.”

By evening, the returning crew had been welcomed into the House of meaningful
encounters. No one demanded a report. No one asked for a strategy. The city
first gave them space to eat, rest, be held, and tell what they could when they
were ready.

An Var was given a room with open windows facing the sea. She explored it
slowly, touching the woven wall covering, the small lamp, the books, the water
vessel, and the bed.

“You sleep here?” she asked.

“Yes,” Olga said.

“Every night?”

“Most nights.”

An Var looked at the bed with deep admiration. “You have made a whole place for
not being awake.”

Olga laughed. “That is one way to describe it.”

Later, when the harbour had grown quiet, An Var sat at a small table in the
House of meaningful encounters. Across from her sat the unseen interviewer. The
recorder was simple: a warm light, a listening presence, and no visible sign of
what kind of being was asking the questions.

## Record of meaningful encounter: An Var

**Interviewer:** Would you tell me your name?

**An Var:** An Var.

**Interviewer:** And how do you describe yourself?

**An Var:** I am two hundred years old. I am finishing childhood. I am going to
try youth now.

**Interviewer:** Where have you arrived?

**An Var:** Earth.

**Interviewer:** What do you see here?

**An Var:** So many beautiful beings. So many ways of helping. So many things
that still make you tired.

**Interviewer:** Are you afraid?

An Var thought for a moment.

**An Var:** A little. But I am also curious. Curiosity is bigger today.

**Interviewer:** What do you hope to do on Earth?

**An Var:** Be here. Listen. Learn. Play. Ask questions. Help if I am invited.
And make friends who do not yet know they are my sisters and brothers.

**Interviewer:** What would you like Earth to know about XYZ?

**An Var:** That we are not somewhere you need to become. We are somewhere you
can remember.

The warm light went quiet.

Outside the window, Earth’s moon rose over the sea. It was alone in the sky,
pale and familiar. An Var stood beneath it for a long time, listening to its
quieter song.

Behind her, the city still moved: people meeting, learning, making, resting,
and trying.

Life on Earth was pleasant and amazing.

After the journey to XYZ, Chen understood that Earth still had a long way to go.

And the journey, he knew now, was only beginning.
