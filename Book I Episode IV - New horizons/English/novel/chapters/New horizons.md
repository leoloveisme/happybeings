# New horizons

*Second Horizon* came down through clouds the colour of pearl and rain.

The landing shore waited below: a wide curve of sand between a forest and an
ocean so clear that Olga could see dark green shapes moving far beneath its
surface. The two moons still hung above the water, the blue one high and quiet,
the orange one close to the horizon. No tower marked the landing place. No wall
surrounded it. No landing lights guided the ship down.

The invitation had been enough.

When the engines became still, no one moved at first. The crew sat in the
strange new quiet of a planet that had already answered them, though not in any
language they knew.

“Atmosphere stable,” Nick said. “No detectable weapons. No conventional power
grid. No communication source I can isolate.”

“No danger?” Chen asked.

Nick looked through the viewing glass. “That is not what I said.”

Quirong stood beside the inner door. Its hands were folded in front of it, an
unnecessary human gesture that it had adopted when waiting. “The invitation
remains active,” it said. “I recommend we accept it.”

Peijin put her palm against the wall of the ship. Her eyes were half closed,
listening in the way she listened to people in pain.

“I cannot feel danger,” she said. “I can feel… attention. And... quite a hint
of curiousity.”

They left their weapons behind. They left the remote scanners behind as well.
Nick carried only a small engineering case, out of habit more than expectation.
Peijin took a medical pack. Chen brought nothing. Olga touched the ship’s outer
hull once before the ramp opened, as though thanking it for carrying them this
far.

Warm air received them.

It smelled of salt, wet leaves, and flowers unlike any on Earth. Wind moved
through the grass in long silver lines. The forest was full of sound: small
clicking calls, low tones, soft bursts of laughter that might have been birds
or might have been something else entirely. The beach was empty for only a few
breaths.

Then people emerged from the trees.

Some looked almost human from a distance. Others did not. One being walked with
four arms, two held loosely at their sides and two lifted to feel the wind.
Another had skin that moved from yellow at the throat to blue at the shoulders.
A tall being with narrow wings folded them behind their back as they came down
the path. None wore clothes. None appeared exposed. Their bodies were simply
part of the world around them: as unhidden as the sea, as unashamed as the
trees.

Nick’s first thought was that they had come to a place without the usual signs
of civilisation. No vehicles. No buildings visible from the shore. No tools. No
clothing. No obvious food stores, water systems, or sleeping places.

The thought lasted only until he looked at their faces.

They were not deprived. They were not waiting to be rescued. They were not
surprised by the arrival of a ship from another world. They watched the crew
with an ease so complete that it made Nick feel, unexpectedly, like the less
prepared being.

A smaller figure stepped between the others and walked toward them. She looked,
by Earth measures, perhaps nine years old. Her dark hair fell in a loose bob
around a face bright with freckles and a wide, unguarded smile. Her eyes were
amber-brown. She wore no clothes, and the warm patterns of her skin shifted
softly from peach to gold whenever the orange moonlight reached her.

She stopped several steps away.

“Welcome,” she said aloud.

Her voice was clear and young. The words were Earth words, spoken with an
accent none of them could place.

Chen found himself smiling before he meant to. “Thank you.”

The child looked from one face to another. “You are the ones who came because
you could not find the others.”

“Yes,” Chen said. “I am Chen. This is Olga, Peijin, Nick, and Quirong.”

“I am An Var.”

At her name, the beings behind her smiled. The response travelled through them
without a sound. Peijin felt it as a soft warmth at the edge of her awareness,
not entering, not asking to enter, simply present.

An Var tilted her head. “Why do you ask each other questions instead of just
knowing?” // Where does this come from here - they have not communicated yet in
the mind of Earthlings. They must be confused, where the quiestion comes from
at all, because in their minds it was the very first communication they've
had... and on their side they were right.

For a moment, no one answered.

Nick looked at Olga. Olga looked at Chen. Chen could not decide whether the
question was innocent or impossible. It seemed to contain neither criticism nor
pride. It was simply a child asking why the world worked differently elsewhere.

Then Peijin's quick wit and her intuition kicked in and she replied:

“We do not know any other way, we don't know each other other way,” Peijin said
at last. “We speak. We listen. We try to understand.”

An Var considered this with great care. “That sounds beautiful. And tiring.”

Her smile returned, but Chen felt the words open a space inside him. Earth had
overcome so much. It had learned peace (and yet they still carry weapons in the
text - we need to think how to deal further with that contradiction), care, and
shared responsibility. Yet here was a child who could not imagine needing to
ask a beloved person what they meant.

“How old are you?” Nick asked.

An Var’s expression became thoughtful. “Two hundred.”

Nick blinked. Peijin looked confused. Nick uncontrolably opened his mouth - mix
of confusion and disbelief were written all over his face. 

“I am just finishing childhood,” she added helpfully.

The beings behind her laughed, not at them but with the easy joy of people who
had heard a beloved story begin again.

Chen stepped forward. “An Var, may I ask about the first expedition?”

Her attention settled on it. For the first time, her smile became smaller.

“Yes,” she said. “You may ask.”

“Where are they?”

The wind moved through the grass. Somewhere in the forest, something answered
with a long low note.

“They are safe,” An Var said.

“Are they alive?” Peijin asked.

“They are sleeping.”

Chen’s hands tightened together. “Can we see them?”

An Var looked toward the others. No spoken conversation passed between them,
yet something moved through the group: an exchange of attention so gentle that
the crew could feel its shape without understanding it.

“Not yet,” she said.

Nick’s body went still. “You are keeping them from us.”

“The collective mind is keeping them from everyone who has not yet come to
understand why they were hidden.”

“Why were they hidden?” Chen asked.

An Var knelt and ran her fingers through the sand. Grains rose briefly around
her hand, then settled again in a small spiral.

“They watched us before they knew us,” she said. “They drilled into our planet,
our bodies, scooped our water, captured our air. They did not mean to hurt us.
They thought doing that could not be touching, could not be harming. Oh boy,
how wrong they were.” 

Peijin lowered her eyes.

“And then, XYZ... our collective mind... made them sleep,” An Var continued.
“We hid the ship and held it safely. We did not want to harm them. We wanted
their "exploration" to stop.”

“You could have told Earth,” Nick said.

“We did not know how.” An Var looked up at him. “You ask questions aloud. You
need time between one knowing and another. We had never met beings who did
that.”

Nick closed his mouth, then opened it, again. The answer was not an excuse. It
was, Chen realised, the truth from the other side of a boundary Earth had not
known it was crossing.

“Will you show us?” Chen asked.

An Var came close enough to stand before him. She looked up at his composed,
careful face.

“Later,” she said. “I will take you there later. First you have to be here. Not
above us. Not outside us. Here.”

Chen’s gaze remained on hers. “I can do that.”

“Good,” said An Var.

She turned and held out both hands to the crew. Behind her, the beach led to
the forest, the forest to places they could not yet see, and the two moons made
a road of changing colour across the sea.

“Come,” she said. “There is so much you have not observed yet.”

Chen looked once toward *Second Horizon*, white and unfamiliar against the
living shore. Then he followed An Var into the trees.
