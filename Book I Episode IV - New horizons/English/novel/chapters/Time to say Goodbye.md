# Time to say "Goodbye"

"We are originally from Earth." said A Ram. 
"Long long time ago we were transported here"
The last morning on XYZ arrived with both moons still visible.

*******
The blue moon had not yet gone below the western hills. The orange moon was pale in the new sun, almost yellow, hovering above the sea like a second dawn. The shore around *Second Horizon* was full of movement: Earth crew members, the beings awakened from *First Horizon*, and XYZ friends who had come simply to be present.

The whole previous expedition and all of our crew beside Str So Fle will go back to Earth tomorrow. 
No one hurried.

***********
The first expedition had been brought from its hidden place the previous evening. Its robots, androids, and cyborgs had woken carefully, one by one, held in the warmth of the collective mind until they could understand where they were and how long Earth had been waiting. Now they stood near the ship, disoriented but safe. Quirong had spoken with each of them for a long time. Then it had returned to Ya Sha’s side.

It was not returning with them.

Str So Fle stood where Quirong had once stood, tall and organic beneath the changing light. The blue of their shoulders softened into silver at their hands. Ya Sha’s fingers were threaded through theirs. Every so often, Str So Fle looked at the wind as if it had just told a joke.

Chen found A Ram near the waterline.

“I did not expect the goodbye to feel like this,” Chen said.

“Like what?”

“Like leaving home and finding home at the same time.”

A Ram smiled. “That is because you have remembered that Earth and XYZ are relatives.”

Chen watched a small group of XYZ children running in and out of the shallows. “You told us that you came from Earth.”

“We did.”

“Long ago?”

“Long enough that some stories became myths, and some myths became forgotten.” A Ram looked across the sea. “A group of Earth beings learned to live without eating, drinking, or sleeping. They called themselves autonomous. They changed, and then they disappeared from the sight of the world they had come from.”

“They did not disappear,” Chen said.

“No. They arrived.”

The answer made him laugh quietly. It also made him want to cry.

Behind them, An Var was speaking with Olga. The two of them had been together often since the first day: walking, asking questions, sitting in long silences that did not need filling. Olga knelt so that she and An Var were at the same height.

“Are you ready to go home?” Olga asked.

An Var looked toward the ship, then toward the forest. “I want to go with you.”

Olga’s face changed. “To Earth?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

An Var smiled. “I am finishing childhood. It is time for youth. Time for trying and experimenting.”

“Earth is not an experiment.”

“No,” An Var said. “It is a beautiful world full of beings who are trying very hard. I want to know what it feels like to find harmony instead of simply living in it.”

Olga looked toward A Ram. “Is this allowed?”

An Var laughed. “Everything based on love is allowed. But we still ask each other.”

They walked together to the open circle of stone where the XYZ beings gathered. There was no formal vote. No one raised a hand. An Var stood in the centre, and every being present gave their attention. The circle grew quiet enough for the crew to hear the sea drawing back from the shore.

An Var spoke aloud for them.

“I want to go to Earth. I want to learn from beings who speak to know one another. I want to see their cities, their Houses of meaningful encounters, their water, their dances, and their ways of remembering. I want to be with Olga, Chen, Peijin, Nick, and the other beings who came here.”

The answer came without words. It crossed the circle like sunlight moving over water. An Var closed her eyes and smiled.

“They say yes,” she said.

Nick’s first response was practical. “Earth will need to prepare. You will need places, people, time, and every kind of support.”

“Then Earth can prepare,” An Var said.

Peijin crouched beside her. “And if you want to come home?”

“I can come home.”

“Good.”

Str So Fle approached the crew at last. For a moment, no one spoke. Chen remembered Quirong’s careful, even voice. He remembered how it had stood at the ship’s ramp and calculated the missing four per cent of departure readiness. The being before him held those memories, but something else had opened behind their eyes.

“I am staying,” Str So Fle said.

“We know,” Chen replied.

“I wanted to say it myself.”

Ya Sha came to stand beside them. There was no need to explain her presence. The choice was visible in the way Str So Fle turned toward her, as natural as a plant turns toward light.

“I am staying because I want to learn this life,” Str So Fle said. “And because I love Ya Sha.”

Ya Sha’s smile was bright and unguarded. “And I love you.”

Str So Fle looked at each member of the crew. “I remember bringing you here. Thank you for bringing me here.”

They embraced Chen first. Their body was warm, flexible, and impossibly soft. Chen found himself laughing through tears at the surprise of it.

“You are very soft,” he said.

“I wanted to be,” Str So Fle answered.

They hugged Olga, Peijin, and Nick in turn. Nick held on longest. When he finally let go, he did not look embarrassed.

The gifts began without anyone naming them gifts.

Col An placed both hands over Chen’s heart and shared the feeling of running under the two moons. Sai Ya touched Peijin’s forehead with hers and offered the memory of a warm current carrying two swimmers toward shore. A Ram gave Olga the remembered certainty that she had always been more than what she was designed to do. Ya Sha placed one hand in Nick’s and showed him, only for a breath, the pleasure of a system that belonged to no machine and yet cared for everyone.

An Var gave nothing visible. She simply took each of their hands in turn.

“I will see Earth through you until I see it myself,” she said.

When the ramp of *Second Horizon* opened, the shore did not become sad. There were tears, embraces, and long looks. But no one spoke as if the distance ahead were a loss. There would be visits. There would be messages, in whatever forms could cross the space between their worlds. There would be transformation.

At the top of the ramp, An Var turned back toward XYZ. The collective attention of the planet seemed to rest around her like a cloak of warm air.

“Goodbye,” she said.

Then she laughed. “Or: see you in every possible way.”

The Earth crew, the first expedition, and An Var entered the ship.

Str So Fle and Ya Sha remained on the shore, holding each other beneath the blue and orange moons.
